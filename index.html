Tab 2 · The main issue

Nevada is running short on water

Southern Nevada gets most of its water from Lake Mead, and the Colorado River that fills it is shrinking.

1,040.4 ftLake Mead’s level on Aug. 7, 2026, its lowest since it filled about 90 years ago
< 2%Nevada’s share of the Colorado River, the smallest of any state in the basin
36%Less Lake Mead water used in Southern Nevada than two decades ago, even as the region grew
Jan. 1, 2027Nevada law bans Colorado River water on nonfunctional grass at most non-single-family properties
Why it’s a problem

Less river, more demand

Years of overuse, drought, and rising temperatures have drained Lake Mead and Lake Powell. The seven states that share the river haven’t agreed on how to divide the shortfall, and current guidelines expire at the end of 2026.

Where the water goes

Mostly outdoors

The Southern Nevada Water Authority says outdoor landscape watering is the region’s largest category of water use. That’s why grass removal is a major focus.

It’s statewide

Not just Las Vegas

Northern and rural Nevada face their own pressures, including a shrinking Walker Lake and fights over groundwater, desert springs and wetlands.

Why you should care: everyone in the desert shares the same limited supply, and small choices at home and in our communities add up.

Formed by environmentalists, tribes, farmers and ranchers to protect groundwater, desert springs and wetlands in one of the driest regions in the country. It relies on volunteers.

Tab 5 · Our digital campaign

How we use our digital influence to amplify them

Our answer: use social media to send people to real actions, one clear ask at a time, and measure results, not likes.

  1. 1
    Weeks 1–2, co-create. Ask each organization what limits its impact most (money, volunteers, or awareness) and get approval on facts, photos and wording before posting.
  2. 2
    Weeks 3–4, build. Make a shared Desert Water Pledge page and 6–8 posts. Line up 2–3 local partners, like schools, shops or creators.
  3. 3
    Weeks 5–8, launch. Every post points to one link with two options: take the free pledge, or support one of the organizations. Host or promote a real event and ask everyone to bring a friend.
  4. 4
    Week 9, report back. Share results, thank supporters, and keep sign-ups on an email list the organizations own.

Frequently asked questions

Why is Lake Mead so low?

Years of overuse, drought and rising temperatures have drained Lake Mead and Lake Powell. It hit its lowest level since it filled about 90 years ago in August 2026.

How much Colorado River water does Nevada get?

Less than 2%, the smallest share of any state in the basin. Southern Nevada has also cut its Lake Mead water use by 36% over two decades.

What is nonfunctional turf, and what changes in 2027?

It’s decorative grass that isn’t used for recreation or other needs. From Jan. 1, 2027, Colorado River water can’t be used to irrigate it on properties not zoned exclusively for a single-family home.

Do I have to remove my lawn at home?

Single-family yards are exempt from the 2027 turf law, but you can still choose to replace grass and apply for a rebate.

What are the current watering rules?

Winter limits of one day per week began Nov. 1 and last through Feb. 28. Look up your assigned day at snwa.com.

How much is the grass rebate?

When we checked in October 2026, single-family homeowners could get $5 per square foot for the first 10,000 square feet. Rates change, so confirm on snwa.com.

How do I report water waste?

Call your water provider, such as 702-822-8571 for the Las Vegas Valley Water District, or use its online form or app. See the How to Help tab for more numbers.

Is the Southern Nevada Conservancy a water agency?

No. It’s a nonprofit that partners with the Bureau of Land Management at Red Rock Canyon on education and volunteer programs. SNWA is the water agency.

What does the Walker Basin Conservancy do?

It leads the effort to restore and maintain Walker Lake near Reno and offers education and community programs.

Can students volunteer with these groups?

It depends on each group’s age rules. Ask the organization first, and get a parent or teacher’s approval.
